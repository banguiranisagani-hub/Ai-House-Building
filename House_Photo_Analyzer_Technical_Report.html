1. Executive Summary & Purpose

The House Photo Analyzer module enables end-users to upload either a real architectural perspective photo or a technical CAD blueprint drawing. The system automatically inspects the visual document, extracts spatial parameters (stated area, outer CAD dimensions, room configurations, and floor storeys), and generates a fully compliant Bill of Materials (BOM), labor allocation schedule, and regional financial valuation.

Key Interface Enhancement: When the Photo Analyzer is active (activeHouse === 'custom_photo'), the AI-generated floor plan blueprint box is automatically removed, allowing the user's uploaded document to occupy 100% full width with dedicated one-click printing and high-resolution zoom capabilities. Preset recommended houses preserve their standard side-by-side AI blueprint view.

2. Visual Document Processing Pipeline

The processing architecture operates across four systematic stages:

Stage 1: Document Classification (Blueprint vs. House Photo)

The system distinguishes technical architectural drawings from real house exterior photos using a dual-channel heuristic and token matching algorithm:

Blueprint Indicators: Tokens such as floor plan, elevation, section a-a, foundation plan, roof plan, scale 1:75, scale 1:50, porch, bedroom, and bath.
Decision Logic: If two or more architectural tags or ratio scales are detected, the document is classified as isBlueprintUploaded = true. Otherwise, it is treated as an architectural facade photo.

Stage 2: How the Floor Area (Square Meters / m²) is Calculated

The system determines the size of the house in square meters (m²) using a clear 4-step priority process:

  • Priority 1 — Stated Written Area:

    The AI first looks for any written numbers that specify the house area in the text or on the image (for example: "60 sqm", "60 sq m", "60 square meters", or "60 m²").

    Example: If the image or title says "60 square meters", the AI automatically sets the floor area to 60 m².
  • Priority 2 — Blueprint Boundary Dimensions (Length × Width):

    When an architectural blueprint is uploaded without a stated total area, the AI reads the outer measurement lines (for example, a width of 6.00 meters and a length of 7.50 meters). It multiplies these two dimensions to get the total floor area:

    Total Floor Area = Width × Length = 6.00 meters × 7.50 meters = 45.0 m²
  • Priority 3 — Room-by-Room Measurement Sum:

    If outer border dimensions are not written, the AI reads the dimensions of each individual room shown on the floor plan (such as Bedroom: 3.00m × 3.00m = 9 m² and Bathroom: 1.50m × 2.50m = 3.75 m²). It adds up all the room sizes and adds 25% extra space to account for the living area, kitchen, hallways, and walls.

    Estimated Area = (Sum of All Room Sizes) + 25% Hallway & Wall Allowance
  • Priority 4 — Shape & Proportions Fallback:

    If the photo has no written measurements at all, the AI analyzes the visual width and height proportions of the house to provide an accurate preliminary estimate based on standard residential building sizes.

Stage 3: How Floor Storeys & Rooms are Detected

The system determines the number of floors, bedrooms, and bathrooms using direct visual and text recognition:

  • Number of Storeys (1-Floor, 2-Floor, or 3-Floor):

    The AI checks for stairs or upper floor levels:

    • If it detects stairs going upward, or text mentioning "2nd floor", "two storey", or "second level", it identifies the house as a 2-Storey House.
    • If it detects upper-level windows positioned directly above the ground-floor windows, it confirms the presence of a second or third floor.
    • If it detects "3rd floor" or 3 stacked window levels, it identifies the house as a 3-Storey House.
  • Bedrooms & Bathrooms Count:

    The AI reads room tags such as "Bedroom 1", "Bedroom 2", "Master Bedroom", and "Bath" or "T&B". It is specially trained so architectural drawing scale ratios (like Scale 1:75) are never mistaken for bedroom numbers.

4. Construction Duration & Workforce Modeling

Project execution timeline is calibrated according to structural complexity and active crew allocation:

Base Duration (Days) = 55 + (Area × 0.45) × Floors

Dynamic crew scaling applies efficiency coefficients for active labor teams:

Crew Composition Effective Duration Formula Trade Allocation
2 Workers (Baseline) Base Days 1 Mason, 1 General Laborer
4 Workers (Balanced) Base Days × 0.67 1 Lead Carpenter, 1 Mason, 1 Steelman, 1 Helper
6 Workers (Expedited) Base Days × 0.45 1 Foreman, 2 Masons, 1 Electrician/Plumber, 2 Helpers

5. Financial Valuation & Regional Cost Structure

The total project budget reflects localized material and labor pricing in the Davao Region (Panabo & Davao del Sur):

Total Valuation = Materials Cost + Labor Cost + Area Baseline + Permits (5%) + Contingency (7%)
  • Materials Cost: Calculated from individual unit rates (Cement @ ₱265/bag, Steel @ ₱45/kg, CHB @ ₱18/pc, Sand @ ₱1,100/m³, Lumber @ ₱42/bd ft, Roof @ ₱380/m², Paint @ ₱240/L, Fixtures @ ₱3,500/set).
  • Labor Cost: Crew Size × Working Days × ₱650/day.
  • Land Quality Area Tier:
    • Residential(RR): ₱8,000 / m²
    • Commercial(CR): ₱11,000 / m²
    • Industrial(I): ₱6,500 / m²
    • Agricultural(A): ₱1,500 / m²
  • Permits & Clearances: 5% of base engineering cost (LGU, barangay, and building permit fees).
  • Engineering Contingency: 7% of base engineering cost (material price inflation and weather mitigation reserve).
